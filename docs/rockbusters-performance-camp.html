What You'll Work On

The Performance Camp splits your development into three deeply interconnected pillars. Each day on the rock addresses all three, but with a rotating primary theme so you build deliberately rather than randomly.

🧗 Technique & Movement

  • Movement efficiency on steep tufa terrain and big holds
  • Footwork precision and smearing on vertical limestone
  • Body positioning, hip rotation and core tension on overhangs
  • Resting positions and recovery on long endurance routes
  • Clipping positions, rhythm and momentum management

🧠 Mental Performance

  • Managing fear of falling and pressure on hard redpoint attempts
  • Building a repeatable pre-climb performance routine
  • Dealing with mental fatigue
  • Committing to moves above gear without hesitation
  • Separating identity from performance on a project

🎯 Strategy & Tactics

  • Route reading and sequencing on steep, complex routes
  • Redpoint tactics: structuring sessions, managing fatigue, timing attempts
  • Projecting strategy: how to choose, break down and send a project
  • Onsight/flash strategy: information control, pacing, risk management
  • Recognising when to commit vs. when to rest and observe

Program & Daily Flow

This camp doesn't follow a rigid day-by-day schedule. Instead, we run it as a performance lab: every day is adjusted to the weather, conditions, group energy and – most importantly – your personal goals and weaknesses. Together we decide what to focus on so you get the maximum out of your time on rock.

Key elements we mix and match

1. On-the-rock coaching sessions

Real climbing, real feedback, real rock.

  • Real climbing on routes chosen for your level, style and goals.
  • Live feedback on movement, tactics and mental approach while you climb.
  • Option to focus more on onsight/flash or projecting, depending on what you need most.
2. Technique labs

Short, focused work on the specific skills holding you back.

  • Short, focused drills on movement efficiency, footwork, body positioning and resting.
  • Route-specific practice on crux sequences and clipping positions.
  • Individual corrections based on how you move on the wall.
3. Mental performance work

Tools for the head game that you can reuse on any crag.

  • Guided exposure to falls and commitment in a way that feels challenging but safe.
  • Tools to deal with fear, pressure and negative self-talk before and during hard attempts.
  • Simple mental routines you can reuse on any crag after the camp.
4. Strategy & planning sessions

How to attack your projects and keep progressing after the camp.

  • Choosing the right projects and building a realistic plan to attack them.
  • Breaking routes into sections, planning rests and key decision points.
  • Learning how to structure climbing days and weeks to keep progressing, not just getting tired.
5. Video analysis & debriefs

See what really happens on the wall – and fix it.

  • Filming selected attempts to see what really happens on the wall.
  • Joint review with your coach, focused on patterns – not judging.
  • Concrete takeaways and adjustments for your next tries.

Tailored to you, every single day

Every climber arrives with a different history, grade, style and headspace. That's why we spend a lot of time talking, watching you climb and adjusting the plan on the go – not just on day one, but every single day.

  • Before and during the camp we clarify your main goals (grade, style, specific routes, mental blocks).
  • Each day we check in on your energy, skin, motivation and fears – and adapt the focus accordingly.
  • If you need more volume, we give you volume. If you need one big hard try with full support, we build the day around it.
  • You always know why we are doing what we are doing – it's a collaboration, not a fixed schedule.

Example focus tracks (instead of a fixed week plan)

Performance breakthrough track

  • You come with a clear redpoint goal (e.g. first 7c/8a).
  • We build your week around working that route + complementary pitches to support it.
  • Most of the coaching focuses on tactics, efficiency and managing pressure on hard tries.

Onsight/flash confidence track

  • You want to push your onsight/flash grade without freezing or overthinking.
  • We pick a range of onsight-appropriate routes and work on decision making, pacing and commitment.
  • A lot of mental work goes into how you approach the unknown and deal with fear of falling.

Technical rebuild track

  • You feel your technique holds you back more than your strength.
  • The week focuses on movement drills, repetition on specific styles (steep tufa, slabs, vertical…), and lots of feedback.
  • The goal is to leave with cleaner, more efficient movement that you can apply anywhere.

One week or two?

To make it super clear when you're deciding:

1-week option – focused reset & tune-up

  • Great if you can't take more time off but still want structured feedback and new tools.
  • You'll warm up, identify key weaknesses, get specific drills and mental tools, and likely experience a few strong performance days before heading home.

If you're on the fence, our honest recommendation as performance coaches: go for two weeks if you can. That's where the biggest change usually happens.

Climbing Paradise in Rodellar, Spain

Rodellar is a tufa paradise – steep limestone caves carved by the Mascún river gorge in the heart of Aragon, with some of the most sustained and athletic sport climbing in the world. It is the ideal venue for projecting and progression, in spectacular canyon scenery. Spring conditions mean long days on rock with good temperatures and manageable crowds.

[ MAP – Rodellar, Aragon, Spain · Nearest airports: Zaragoza (ZAZ) · Barcelona (BCN) ]

  • Grades ranging from 6a to 9a – ideal for projecting and performance work
  • Warm spring climate with excellent conditions in May – long days, dry rock, manageable temperatures
  • One of the world's great sport climbing destinations – a bucket-list crag for any serious climber

FAQ for This Camp

Is this camp right for me if I only climb indoors at 6c?
Probably not yet. The Performance Lab assumes solid outdoor lead experience and familiarity with bolt-to-bolt climbing on real rock. If you're primarily an indoor climber or haven't yet built confidence on outdoor routes, we'd recommend our Intro to Outdoor Lead or Sport Climbing Beginner Camp first. Come back to the Performance Lab once you have a solid base outdoors.
How scared of falling can I be and still benefit from the camp?
A healthy fear of falling is completely normal and you are not disqualified from the camp because of it. Fall practice and fear management are core parts of the Mental Performance pillar – we work on this systematically and at your pace. What matters is that you're willing to engage with it. Climbers who arrive anxious about falls often have the biggest breakthroughs by the end of the week.
Can I come alone – will I find partners?
Absolutely. Around 70% of Performance Lab participants travel solo. The small group format means you'll be climbing with the same people every day, and the intensity of the program builds real bonds fast. Most people leave as firm climbing partners with plans to meet up again.
What gear do I need to bring?
Your personal climbing kit: shoes (bring two pairs if possible – one for warming up, one for redpoints), harness, chalk bag, belay device. A light rack of quickdraws (10–12) is useful, though the group shares draws on project routes. Bring sport clothing for warm conditions. Rockbusters provides shared safety equipment. And remember – you'll have a 200€ Singing Rock voucher to spend on gear.
What happens if the weather is bad for several days?
Rodellar in May is extremely reliable, but we always have contingency plans. The canyon has sheltered cave sectors that stay dry in light rain. If we genuinely lose climbing days, we use the time for extended video analysis, mental coaching workshops and technique drills on warm-up terrain. We have never run a camp where weather caused significant lost climbing time in May.
Can I choose between 1 week and 2 weeks?
Yes – the camp runs as a 1-week option (May 2–9, €950) or a full 2-week program (May 2–16, €1,650). The 2-week format allows a deeper coaching arc: week one focuses on assessment and technical work, week two shifts entirely to performance and consolidation. For climbers with the time, two weeks produce significantly more measurable progress.
Is the 200€ Singing Rock voucher usable before the camp?
The voucher is provided at the start of the camp and is valid for Singing Rock's full gear range. We recommend using it at or after the camp once Klemen has given you feedback on your gear setup – he may have specific recommendations based on your movement patterns and goals.
Do I need climbing insurance?
Yes – personal climbing and travel insurance with mountain rescue cover is strongly recommended. It is not included in the camp price. We can point you to suitable providers on request.
